Token size

LLMs work with tokens, a sequence of characters rather than words. So multiple tokens can form a word. Importantly for names, case can effect tokens.

1 token    - Sarah
2 tokens - sarah

An LLM like GPT-5 breaks up a word into tokens by how frequently those tokens occur across all the vocabulary in the training data.

So, if a name like "Rowan Hale" occurs very frequently in the training data, it is likely to be turned into a smaller number of tokens. If it rarely occurred it would more tokens.

This gives us a rough idea of how frequently Rowan Hale might have occurred in the training data compared with other Slop names:

2 tokens - Sarah Mitchell
2 tokens - James Carter
3 tokens - Rowan Hale
6 tokens - Aisha Okonkwo

Its worth mentioning OpenAI's tokeniser tool, used for these examples only shows how a word might be tokenized.

Why Rowan Hale?

  • "Rowan" and "Hale" are names from common, open training datasets but the connection between them is not.
  • No other companies' LLM generates the name, something specific to OpenAI's training and refinement pushes this name
  • "Rowan Hale" appears to be prominent in the OpenAI's training data, at a guess this could be from OpenAI using its own LLM to generated training data or an unknown dataset.
  • Rowan being unisex makes it flexible as an answer.
  • Hale being associated with health/strength may make it appealing.
  • Short and easy to pronounce for certain audiences.

OpenAI is an American company and the training data sources are known to amplify English speaking culture (Note: we did not get any names outside of the English American alphabet).

It would be unsurprising that OpenAI, an American company, using training data dominated by American culture would be targeted towards satisfying an American, English speaking audience. Even further someone with disposable income to spend on AI services and an excitement to embrace disruptive Technology.
